Datasources

Your questions address Datasources. A Datasource is defined as a set of 'tables' in a database and selected or configured in the datasource selection dialog that comes up when clicking the database badge in the header.

Out of the box, you're offered various demo datasources hosted in different supported database types. You may create your own datasource by connecting to your own database in the 'Bring Your Own Data' section of Settings - once saved there, it shows up by name in the datasource selection dialog so you can pick it like any other datasource. The application supports several database systems including: postgreSQL, mySQL, Oracle, Microsoft SQL Server, Google BigQuery, Snowflake, Databricks, Amazon Redshift, MongoDB, and Google Sheets. Your custom database connections are kept private and secure. If you do not log in, your custom connections are scoped to your current browser session only - nobody else can see or use them. If you log in, your connections are scoped to your account instead, so they follow you across sessions and devices, but are still never visible to any other user. Sensitive fields - passwords, service-account keys, private keys, access tokens - are encrypted at rest in the App's state database, and are never sent back to your browser once saved.

Composite Datasources

Datasources can be defined as groups of other datasources. Some such datasources are offered out of the box. In future versions, users will be able to define their own composite datasources. For composite datasources, the agent decides which constituent datasource(s) to contact and may also 'blend' (i.e. JOIN or UNION) results from different datasources in its memory when that is necessary in order to answer the user prompt.

Datasources vs. General Knowledge

The agent always prefers to respond based on the information stored in the provided Datasource(s). If that is not possible, the Agent is allowed to respond based on their general knowledge. When appropriate, the Agent 'blends' information from both the provided datasources and general knowledge. For example, responding to the question 'Is the current President of the USA one of my customers?' against an E-Commerce Store datasource, requires this blending. This blending may be 'pushed down' into the queries that fetch results from the related databases or take place as a 'widening' step after the results are back.

Multi-Turn Conversations

The application remembers your last 3 turns. Each turn contains the prompt, the SQL it generated, the results that came back from the database, and any summarization. Use the ◀ / ▶ arrows in the NL prompt text box to step back and forward through previous turns. You may review past turns and delete them in the History Dialog.
